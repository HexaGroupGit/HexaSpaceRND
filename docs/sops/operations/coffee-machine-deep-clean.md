## Purpose

The periodic clean the daily routine doesn't cover — the one that stops a slow decline nobody notices until the coffee is bad and the machine needs a technician.

## When to do this

The **Dr. Coffee Mini Bar prompts for its own cleaning and descaling cycles.** Respond to the prompt rather than working to a calendar — it counts brews and water hardness, which is more accurate than a schedule.

Beyond the prompts, the manual jobs below want doing roughly weekly.

> **NEEDS INPUT:** does it prompt on screen, and roughly how often in practice? And which quiet day/time is the deep clean done?

## Before you start

- The machine is **out of service** while this runs — a full clean or descale cycle takes a while. Put a sign up or members will queue.
- **Three different chemicals, three different jobs.** Do not substitute:

| Product | For |
|---|---|
| **Brew-unit cleaning tablets** | Coffee oils in the brewing group |
| **Milk-system cleaner** | The milk circuit |
| **Descaler** | Limescale in the water circuit |

> **NEEDS INPUT:** which products are used, and where they're kept.

## Steps — brew unit clean

1. Start the machine's **cleaning cycle** and add a **cleaning tablet** when prompted.
2. Let it run fully. It rinses itself afterwards — don't interrupt.
3. **Remove the brew unit** if this model allows it, rinse under warm running water, let it drain, and refit.
   > **NEEDS INPUT:** is the Mini Bar's brew group user-removable? On many Dr. Coffee models it is, behind a side or front panel. Confirm before anyone forces a panel.
4. **Never put the brew unit in the dishwasher** and don't use detergent on it — warm water only. Detergent residue taints every coffee afterwards.

## Steps — milk system deep clean

5. Run the **milk-system clean** with cleaner (the same cycle as end-of-day, but this time strip the parts too).
6. **Remove the milk tube, connectors and frother parts**, soak them in cleaning solution, and brush through.
7. Rinse everything thoroughly and refit, checking each connection seats properly.
8. Run a milk-only test and taste it — soured milk residue is instantly obvious.

## Steps — grinder and hopper

9. **Empty the hopper** and wipe the inside. Coffee oil goes rancid and is a very common cause of "the beans taste off".
10. Brush out the chute and around the burrs.
11. **Never wash the grinder or the burrs with water.** Dry brush and a cloth only.

## Steps — descale

Only when the machine asks, and only with actual descaler.

12. Start the **descale cycle** and follow the on-screen prompts — it will ask for descaler and water, and pause for rinses.
13. **Let it finish.** Interrupting a descale part-way leaves acid in the circuit.
14. **Rinse thoroughly** after. Descaler residue tastes appalling and members will tell you.
15. Discard the first couple of coffees and a milk drink before serving.

> **NEEDS INPUT:** is there an inline water filter? If so, type and replacement interval — a filter reduces descaling frequency considerably.

## After

16. **Pull a test coffee and taste it.** If there's any chemical note, rinse again and re-test.
17. **Record it** — date and what was done.
    > **NEEDS INPUT:** where? A log sheet by the machine, or a Maintenance entry. Recording is what makes the schedule real rather than aspirational.

## Common mistakes

- **Substituting one chemical for another.** Descaler will not clean coffee oils; tablets will not remove scale.
- **Interrupting a cycle.** Descale especially — it leaves acid in the machine.
- **Detergent or the dishwasher on the brew unit.** Taints everything afterwards.
- **Water on the grinder burrs.**
- **Not rinsing enough.** The most common complaint after a deep clean is chemical taste.
- **Doing it at 9am.**
- **Not recording it.** Without a record nobody knows if it's been a week or two months.

## If something goes wrong

- **Still tastes of chemicals** — keep rinsing and re-testing. It will clear.
- **The brew unit won't refit** — don't force it. It seats one way; check its alignment mark, or leave it and call the technician.
- **Descale cycle stops mid-way** — do not serve from it. Restart the cycle; if it won't complete, escalate.
- **Leaking after reassembly** — a seal isn't seated. Off at the wall, log it, escalate.
- **Anything you had to force** — stop. A stripped fitting costs more than a service call.
