## Purpose

Handle someone arriving who isn't a member, and handle a member who isn't happy.

## Visitors and guests

### Steps

1. **Greet and ask who they're here to see.**
2. **Let the member know.** Message or call — don't send a visitor wandering.
3. **Sign them in.**
   > **NEEDS INPUT:** is there a visitor book, a tablet, or nothing? Fire safety usually requires knowing who's in the building.
4. **Escort or direct** them, depending on where they're going.
5. **Visitors don't get door access.** The member meets them or lets them through.

### Someone who wants to work here today

That's a **drop-in**, not a visitor. They pay before they get space — see [Take a drop-in booking](../bookings/drop-in-booking.md). Don't seat someone "just for an hour" without processing it; that's exactly the gap the pay-on-the-spot rule closed.

### Someone enquiring about space

Log it as a lead while they're standing there — see [Log a walk-in enquiry](../front-of-house/log-an-enquiry.md). Offer a tour if you can; book one if you can't.

### Deliveries and couriers

Log at reception — see [Receive and release mail](../front-of-house/receive-and-release-mail.md). The member is emailed automatically.

> **NEEDS INPUT:** are couriers allowed past reception? And is there a rule about large or refrigerated deliveries?

## Complaints

### The shape of it

1. **Listen properly.** Let them finish. Most complaints are 80% wanting to be heard.
2. **Don't defend the space in the first breath.** Acknowledge it first — "that shouldn't have happened" costs nothing and changes the conversation.
3. **Write it down in front of them.** It signals it's going somewhere.
4. **Say what happens next and when.** A vague "I'll look into it" is worse than "I'll come back to you by Thursday".
5. **Log it** — Maintenance for a physical fault, a note on the member for anything else.
6. **Actually come back to them**, even if the answer is no.

### What you can settle yourself

- Something broken → log it, give a timeframe
- A cleaning miss → fix it now
- A booking clash → find them another room
- A one-off inconvenience → an apology and a fix

### What goes to Eric

- Anything about **money** — a refund, a credit, a fee waiver
- Anything about their **contract** or notice
- A complaint about **another member**
- Anything threatening a **claim** or mentioning legal
- Anything you'd be uncomfortable defending later

Say "I'll get that to Eric today and someone will come back to you" — don't improvise a commercial answer.

### Never

- **Never promise a refund or a discount.** Not yours to give, and hard to walk back.
- **Never blame another member** to the person complaining.
- **Never argue about what's in the House Rules** in the moment. Note it, check, come back.

## Common mistakes

- **Fixing without recording.** Three complaints about the same thing look like one grumble.
- **Defending before acknowledging.**
- **Promising a timeframe you won't meet.** Under-promise.
- **Letting a walk-in work for free** because they seemed nice.
- **Not telling the member their visitor arrived.**

> **NEEDS INPUT:** is there a complaints escalation beyond Eric, or a formal process for a member dispute? And do the House Rules cover guest conduct?
