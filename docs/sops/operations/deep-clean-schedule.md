## Purpose

The periodic clean beyond the daily routine — the things that only look bad once they've been neglected for a month.

## When to do this

> **NEEDS INPUT:** the actual cadence, and who does each item — in-house staff, the regular cleaners, or a specialist contractor. That split is the substance of this SOP.

## Suggested structure

The scaffold below is a starting shape, not a decision. Adjust it to what you actually do.

### Weekly
- Coffee machine deep clean — see [Coffee machine — deep clean](coffee-machine-deep-clean.md)
- Fridge clear-out and wipe
- Meeting room glass and whiteboards
- Skirting, sills and behind the lounge furniture
- End-of-trip facilities: showers scrubbed, drains checked

### Monthly
- Carpet spot-clean in high-traffic runs
- Vents and air-con grilles
- Under and behind the kitchen units
- Windows, internal glass and partitions
- Bin store

### Quarterly
- Carpet steam clean
- Upholstery
- Coffee machine descale, if not more frequent
- Light fittings
- External windows

> **NEEDS INPUT:** which of these are actually in your cleaning contract, and which fall to staff? Anything neither party owns is the thing that never gets done.

## Steps

1. Book it when the space is quietest.
   > **NEEDS INPUT:** best day/time, and whether members need warning.
2. If it affects a shared space, tell members ahead — an **Announcement** takes a minute and prevents complaints. See [Post an announcement](../front-of-house/post-an-announcement.md).
3. Do the work, or supervise the contractor.
4. **Record it** under **Maintenance**, with what was done and when.
5. Walk the area afterwards and check it against what was asked for.

## Why recording matters

Without a record, "when was the carpet last done?" has no answer, and the schedule quietly becomes whatever anyone remembers. Logging each deep clean under Maintenance turns it into something you can audit — and gives you evidence when a contractor's work slips.

## Common mistakes

- **Deep-cleaning a shared space with no warning.** Members turn up to a room they can't use.
- **Assuming the cleaners do it.** Check the contract; the gaps are where the grime lives.
- **Not checking the work.** Contractors do what's inspected, not what's asked.
- **No record.** The schedule becomes fiction.
- **Doing it in business hours** because it's convenient for staff.

## If something goes wrong

- **A contractor no-shows** — reschedule and note it. A pattern is a contract conversation.
- **Damage during a clean** — photograph it, log it under Maintenance, and raise it with the contractor the same day.
- **A member complains mid-clean** — apologise, give them a timeframe, and offer another space to work in.

> **NEEDS INPUT:** cleaning contractor name, contact and what their contract covers. Attach or reference the scope so the boundary is documented, not remembered.
